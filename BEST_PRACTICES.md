## General Guidelines

### Be Clear and Focused

- **Choose a specific topic**: Narrow briefs are more useful than broad overviews
- **Define your scope**: State what your brief covers (and doesn't cover)
- **Target an audience**: Know who will benefit from this research

### Prioritize Accuracy

- **Verify AI outputs**: Don't blindly trust AI-generated content
- **Cross-check facts**: Use multiple reputable sources
- **Update outdated information**: Research evolves; keep briefs current
- **Acknowledge limitations**: Be honest about uncertainties

### Add Value

- **Synthesize information**: Don't just copy-paste; add analysis
- **Provide context**: Explain why this research matters
- **Highlight key insights**: Help readers quickly grasp important points
- **Include practical applications**: Show real-world relevance

## Crafting Good Prompts

The quality of your brief starts with your prompt.

### Good Prompts Are:

**Specific**
```
❌ "Tell me about climate change"
✅ "Summarize the latest IPCC findings on ocean acidification and its impact on marine ecosystems"
```

**Well-Structured**
```
✅ "Create a research brief that:
1. Explains the current state of quantum computing
2. Lists major breakthroughs in the last 2 years
3. Identifies key challenges remaining
4. Discusses potential applications in cryptography"
```

**Context-Rich**
```
✅ "For an audience of undergraduate biology students, explain CRISPR gene editing, including:
- Basic mechanisms
- Current applications
- Ethical considerations
- Recent developments (2023-2024)"
```

### Prompting Techniques

1. **Use Role-Playing**: "As an expert in [field], explain..."
2. **Request Structure**: "Organize this into sections: Introduction, Main Findings, Implications"
3. **Specify Depth**: "Provide a detailed analysis" vs "Give a high-level overview"
4. **Ask for Examples**: "Include 2-3 real-world examples"
5. **Request Comparisons**: "Compare approach A vs approach B"

## Structuring Your Brief

### Essential Sections

**1. Title**
- Clear and descriptive
- Include key terms for searchability
- Keep under 100 characters

**2. Abstract**
- 150-300 words
- Summarize main points
- Mention key findings or conclusions
- Help readers decide if this brief is relevant

**3. Introduction/Context**
- Why this topic matters
- Background information
- Scope and limitations

**4. Main Content**
- Organized into logical sections
- Use headings and subheadings
- Include bullet points for readability
- Add emphasis to key insights

**5. Conclusion/Summary**
- Key takeaways
- Implications
- Future directions

**6. Sources/References**
- List all sources consulted
- Use proper citation format
- Link to original research when possible

### Optional Sections

- **Methodology**: How you researched this topic
- **Case Studies**: Real-world examples
- **FAQ**: Common questions addressed
- **Further Reading**: Additional resources
- **Glossary**: Define technical terms

## Citations and Sources

### Always Cite When You:

- Quote directly from a source
- Paraphrase someone else's ideas
- Present specific data or statistics
- Reference a specific study or paper
- Build upon existing research

### Citation Best Practices

**Use Reputable Sources**
- Peer-reviewed journals
- Academic institutions
- Government agencies
- Established news organizations
- Domain experts

**Provide Complete Information**
```
✅ Smith, J. (2024). "Neural Network Advances."
   Nature Machine Learning, 15(3), 234-245.
   https://doi.org/10.1038/...

❌ Smith study on AI
```

**Mix Source Types**
- Primary research papers
- Review articles
- Books
- Reports
- Expert commentary

**Keep Sources Current**
- Prioritize recent research (last 2-5 years)
- Note publication dates
- Update briefs as new research emerges

## Formatting and Style

### Enhance Readability

**Use Hierarchy**
```markdown
# Main Title (H1)
## Major Sections (H2)
### Subsections (H3)
```

**Break Up Text**
- Short paragraphs (3-5 sentences)
- Bullet points for lists
- Numbered lists for steps/sequences
- Tables for comparisons
- Block quotes for key statements

**Add Visual Elements**
- Use bold for **key terms**
- Use italics for *emphasis*
- Use code blocks for technical content
- Consider diagrams (when supported)

### Writing Style

**Be Concise**
- Remove filler words
- Use active voice
- One idea per sentence
- Get to the point quickly

**Be Clear**
- Define technical terms
- Avoid jargon when possible
- Use simple language
- Explain acronyms on first use

**Be Objective**
- Present facts, not opinions
- Acknowledge different viewpoints
- Avoid sensationalism
- Maintain neutral tone

## Quality Assurance

### Before Publishing

**Review for Accuracy**
- [ ] Facts are correct and current
- [ ] Sources are properly cited
- [ ] Claims are supported by evidence
- [ ] No plagiarism or copyright issues

**Check Structure**
- [ ] Logical flow of information
- [ ] All sections are complete
- [ ] Headings are descriptive
- [ ] Consistent formatting

**Improve Clarity**
- [ ] Technical terms are defined
- [ ] Sentences are clear and concise
- [ ] No grammatical errors
- [ ] No spelling mistakes

**Verify Completeness**
- [ ] Abstract summarizes the brief
- [ ] Introduction provides context
- [ ] Conclusion ties everything together
- [ ] All sources are listed

### After Publishing

**Monitor Feedback**
- Read reviews and comments
- Address questions
- Consider suggestions
- Learn from criticism

**Update as Needed**
- Create new versions for major updates
- Fix factual errors immediately
- Add new research as it emerges
- Note what changed in changelog

**Engage with Community**
- Respond to reviews
- Thank contributors
- Share insights in discussions
- Build on others' work

## Common Pitfalls to Avoid

### Content Issues

❌ **Overgeneralizing**
- "All studies show..." → "Most studies suggest..."

❌ **Cherry-picking data**
- Present the full picture, including contrary evidence

❌ **Outdated information**
- Always check publication dates

❌ **Missing context**
- Explain why findings matter

### Structural Issues

❌ **Walls of text**
- Break into paragraphs and sections

❌ **Poor organization**
- Use logical structure

❌ **Burying the lead**
- Important information should come early

### Citation Issues

❌ **Uncited claims**
- Always provide sources for facts

❌ **Vague references**
- "Studies show..." → Cite specific studies

❌ **Broken links**
- Verify all URLs work
